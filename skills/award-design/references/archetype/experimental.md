# Experimental — tier 1

**Voice.** The substrate is chosen first and never left. One bespoke medium — a hand-built physics world, an SDF-shader canvas, a giant-type index over a live WebGL field, a rigid-body tumble field — renders every element class, so button, link, figure, nav and index row each behave differently and still read as one authored world. The site is the medium, hand-coded from primitives; framework templates do not produce this line. Cohesion is manufactured by three constants: one signal that means *live* on every class (a colour accent, or a tonal state where the palette has no accent), one easing family used everywhere, and — on the spatial stack — one positional audio bed as the literal through-line. Variety lives in the per-class mechanic.

**Register licence.** Everything is permitted except illegibility; the excess is structural, not decorative, and the only hard bind is that Awwwards Usability is 30% of the score and judges check mobile first. Lusion v3 takes Developer Site of the Year 2023 with Animations 10.00; Shader Development Studio takes SOTD 2026-04-20 at 7.73 and a Developer Award of 8.12 carrying Animations/Transitions 9.40 *and* Responsive 8.40 — the ceiling belongs to wheel-driven scenes that still answer a thumb. Bruno's Portfolio scores 8.11 overall with Creativity 8.62, and its Developer Award of 7.65 carries Responsive 8.20 on a world where click, scroll, keyboard, touch and gamepad are recreated as 3D raycasts. Restraint binds at two points only: a discoverable anchor delivering understanding in ten seconds, and amplitude peaks capped at one or two replayable ones. The idle band is uncapped — the substrate simulates between inputs, or the build has no archetype.

**Anti-signals — these disqualify.**
- A pale washed-out tint fill on button hover — a 10–20% brand-alpha sweep is the single clearest tell.
- A scrolled solid nav bar with a contrasting `border-bottom`: a corporate pattern that never ports here.
- One identical hover cloned onto every element class; a single CSS trick stamped on an otherwise normal DOM.
- A CSS underline-draw as the link move. That belongs to editorial; this line decodes, scrambles or warps.
- Generic `fade-up 20px` as the only reveal; a `background-clip:text` gradient sold as kinetic type.
- A spinner or a bare percent counter with no tie to the concept, then a hard cut into the page.
- A `mix-blend-mode` lag-dot cursor that adds no mechanic, on a page where the pointer already has a job.
- A card-grid fold, stock photography, a footer sitemap.
- The frozen canvas: a hero shader that stops after boot, a WebGL field that renders on scroll-in then parks. If a still image could replace the canvas without loss, the substrate was never built.

**Macrostructures it runs.**
- `engine-world`, free-roam — no scroll; the funnel jobs collapse into an operable 3D space and landmarks are the nav. Route here when the brief names a verb the visitor performs: drive, walk, pilot. (Bruno's Portfolio, winner-verified)
- `engine-world`, journey — one continuous in-engine scene whose section breaks are shader seams; the wheel drives a camera, not a document. Route here when the brief supplies discrete projects that can each own a zone. (Igloo Inc SOTY 2024; Lusion v3 Developer SOTY 2023)
- `type-index` — a corner counter boots straight into a giant-type index over a live WebGL field; no marketing hero, no prose, and the climax is on-click. Route here when a body of named work is itself the argument. (Aristide Benoist, winner-verified)
- Physics field — elements are rigid bodies with weight and restitution that you grab, throw and tumble; the simulation is the medium and the reward. Route here when the brief's objects have tactile identity. It runs the `engine-world` shape on a 2D engine; the library form is `physics-tumble-field`. (MoMoney, winner-verified)

**Reflexes — enumerate, then reject by name before committing.**
1. A dark page with a full-screen shader-noise background, conventional DOM sections scrolling over it untouched.
2. Three.js boilerplate: a rotating distorted icosahedron or torus knot parked behind the hero headline.
3. A `mix-blend-mode: difference` lag-dot cursor offered as the entire evidence that the build is custom.
4. A particle field that answers the mouse and nothing else, inert for the rest of the page.
5. "Experimental" delivered as an oversized-type marquee plus a glitch or RGB-split filter on hover.
6. A percentage preloader over the canvas, then a hard cut into a conventional landing page underneath.
7. Scroll-jacking presented as a bespoke navigation metaphor, with no anchor telling the visitor what to do.
8. The whole budget spent on the hero canvas; the mid-page reverting to cards, icons and fade-ups.
