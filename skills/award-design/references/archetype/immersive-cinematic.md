# Immersive-cinematic — tier 1

**Voice.** The medium is the message, and it is driven rather than displayed. One real-time surface carries the whole narrative — a 3D scene, rendered terrain, a scroll-scrubbed real sequence, a full-bleed cinematic frame — and the visit moves through it: scroll is a camera, not a reveal schedule. Chrome stays minimal, sections run full-viewport, body copy is title-cards where other lines write paragraphs. Cohesion comes from three constants layered on that surface — one scarce saturated accent that means "active" on every element class, one easing family of two or three named beziers, one gesture grammar (inversion, or reveal-from-edge) whose per-class mechanic differs. Winners pick one surface and never leave it.

**Register licence.** Excess is licensed in the medium and nowhere else. The peak law inverts here: amplitude peaks stay capped at one or two, while the vocabulary of continuation is uncapped — every section keeps the medium driven, and the defining defect is silence, a section whose only motion is décor over a static base. Restraint binds on the palette (one accent), the easing set, the copy, and the budget: cross-device parity is weighted higher here than in any other line because judges check mobile first. Messenger ships 5.7MB init / 17.5MB max on a Three.js playable planet and still takes SOTD 2025-11-10 at 7.92 with Developer Award 8.21 — animations 9.00, responsive 8.40, WPO 8.80. Weight is not the constraint; a dead surface is.

**Anti-signals — these disqualify.**
- A procession of static photographs fading in on scroll with a particle canvas drifting behind: the immersive costume with no medium under it.
- A pale `rgba(accent,.1)` tint fill on a primary control. Winners flood the full token and invert the label, or ship the CTA already solid.
- A frosted-glass nav on scroll, or a nav `border-bottom` of any color — `border-bottom: 0 none` is verified on both live-read winners.
- One universal hover across every element class; or the reverse, a different easing per element.
- Hover amplitude at `scale 1.02–1.03`. The media move is 10%, or it is not felt.
- A bare spinner or a naked percentage counter hard-cutting into the hero, on the most asset-heavy line of the nine.
- A card grid or bento fold, a hero carousel, a stock mosaic, an explanatory hero paragraph.
- A clip set to `loop` whose last frame does not resolve into its first; unmuted autoplay with no costumed gate.

**Macrostructures it runs.**
- `portrait-procession` — one person told through facets over a DOM page, a recurring 3D feature between them, the close returning the opening subject. Route here when the subject is a single personality; the 3D feature requires a WebGL path. (Lando Norris, winner-verified)
- `rooms-procession` — discrete staged 3D alcoves sharing one canvas and one camera rig, scroll transitioning the camera room to room. Route here when the brief supplies a SET of artifacts; requires a WebGL path. (Cartier Watches & Wonders 2025, Hubtown, winner-verified)
- `engine-world` — the engine is the page and there is no "after the hero": a camera Z-dive on one object (Oryzo), an aerial flythrough over rendered terrain (Primland), or a free-roam one-verb world (Messenger, Bruno Simon). Route here when the brief reduces to one object, one place, or one verb. Never commit without a WebGL path. (winner-verified)
- `gated-reel` — a costumed threshold that doubles as the sound unlock, then an operable reel of treated stills. Route here when the brief is a treated archive or a festival. (Siena Film Foundation, winner-verified)
- `studio-reel` — a live-rendered ground under a manifesto line and a discipline-tagged reel. Route here for a studio selling the medium itself; never commit the live-3D hero without a WebGL path. (Lusion v3, winner-verified)

**Reflexes — enumerate, then reject by name before committing.**
1. Near-black page, one neon accent, Monument Grotesk at 200px: the Active Theory screenshot.
2. A stock drone clip full-bleed on `autoplay muted loop` — footage sourced where a medium should have been decided.
3. A primitive lathe-turned object — torus, extruded logo, low-poly bottle — under two point lights, shipped because 3D was the first idea.
4. Scroll hijacking as pacing: a pinned section that eats three wheel turns and pays out one fade.
5. A drifting starfield or particle field standing in for a decision about the world.
6. A 60% black scrim dropped over the whole frame so white type can sit anywhere — legibility traded against the image instead of composed into it.
7. A circle-follower or magnetic-blob cursor added because the page is "immersive", driving nothing.
8. Desktop-first WebGL with a poster image shipped to mobile as the responsive answer.
