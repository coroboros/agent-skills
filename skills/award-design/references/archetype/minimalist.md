# Minimalist — tier 1

**Voice.** Subtraction as conviction. Two to three colors carry the entire system; typography carries hierarchy through scale, weight and color, never through chrome; macro-whitespace (120–200px+ desktop section padding) is active composition. Every element answers "what breaks if this disappears". The single accent is punctuation — present once per viewport, never twice.

**Register licence.** Restraint governs palette, type and chrome — never motion craft, never ambition. Terminal Industries takes SOTM with Animations/Transitions 8.80 as its highest sub-score: this line wins on motion precision, not on stillness. The signature medium is allowed to be rich — a canvas frame-sequence, a scroll-scrubbed real sequence, a WebGL showreel (Pacome Pertant ships a Three.js reel on a two-color monochrome palette, SOTD 2026-06-09, 7.76) — and the restraint stays in the type and the palette. A page that reads still *overall* is the empty-and-dead failure, not the quiet register.

**Anti-signals — these disqualify.**
- Inter, or any retail geometric sans, at default tracking as the display face: type selection standing in for art direction.
- A 5–10% pale-tint wash on a *primary* CTA. Primaries move a full token; the wash is quarantined to ghost buttons.
- One `fade-up 20px, .6s ease` on every section; Tailwind's default `cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1) .15s` left as the site's motion identity.
- A contrasting `border-bottom` under a solid nav bar on scroll — bars float transparent over the hero or frost translucent.
- The same underline-slide smeared across buttons, links and nav. Winners split them: wipe on buttons, draw on links, dot on nav.
- A circle-follower or magnetic-blob cursor added by reflex; a spinner or a blocking brand-color splash.
- Blanding — cookie-cutter sans over a safe muted palette; a second accent visible in one viewport; mixed-grade imagery.
- A bank of fire-once IntersectionObserver reveals, inert on the second pass — which is the pass a jury scrolls.

**Macrostructures it runs.**
- `argument-scroll` — one held claim argued beat by beat, the `<h1>` deferred to the footer. Route here when the brief states one product promise. (Terminal Industries, winner-verified)
- `gallery-stack` — the work is the argument: a type intro assembling over a main gallery of full-bleed figures, route-transition SPA across the detail views. Route here when the brief supplies a body of visual work. (Gabriel Contassot, Stefan Vitasović, winner-verified)
- scene scroll (no catalog slug of its own yet) — a real rendered medium is the signature and the hero transformation is the one loud peak. Route here when the brief has something to show *transforming*, not only to argue. (Terminal's `<canvas>` frame-sequence, Pacome Pertant's Three.js reel)
- `standfirst-stack` — even reading cadence, no spectacle spike, the voice living in the chrome. Route here only when the brief explicitly forbids spectacle.

**Reflexes — enumerate, then reject by name before committing.**
1. Off-white page, one violet accent, Inter, hairline dividers: the Linear/Stripe screenshot.
2. Centered hero — eyebrow, oversized headline, subhead, two stacked CTAs.
3. A stock cinematic photograph standing in for a decision about imagery.
4. The 1→100 counter preloader as ceremony, with no choreographed handoff into the page.
5. Whitespace as the whole idea: no climax, no carry, "clean" delivered as empty.
6. The accent spent twice in one viewport because a single placement felt timid.
7. Three-column feature cards with an icon on top of each.
8. The motion budget spent entirely on section entrances, nothing on the substrate between them.
