# Corporate-luxury — tier 1

**Voice.** Inherited authority. A custom or premium serif at display carries the mark; a quiet grotesk carries the body. Generous whitespace (128–200px+ desktop section padding) reads as exclusivity, and the palette rests on neutral ground punctuated once by a jewel tone, a muted gold, or a single deep house colour. Motion runs long — 1–1.5s under `cubic-bezier(0.25,1,0.5,1)` — and every photograph frames the product as an object, never a commodity. The copy names the specific (a date, a place, a count) and refuses the transaction. The luxury tell is that the effect is felt, not announced.

**Register licence.** Restraint governs palette, type, chrome and hover amplitude — never motion craft, never presence. The line's ceiling sub-scores are motion: Cartier WAW 2025 takes SOTD 2025-08-18 (7.64) with Animations/Transitions 9.00, Louis Vuitton Collectibles SOTD 2024-02-20 (7.79) with Animations 9.4. What actually binds is amplitude, not peak count and not coverage: peaks stay capped at 0–1, and a continuous, low-amplitude, reversible signature thread must run under every viewport hero to footer — a scored 3D drift, a scroll-scrubbed parallax/film bed, a living WebGL displacement ground, or a frosted craft skin. The hero opens the thread; it never closes it. Going silent after the hero is the defect the jury reads as least alive — Delvaux ships the corpus's highest community craft score and takes the lowest award tier in it (HM 2026-06-15).

**Anti-signals — these disqualify.**
- A pale or washed tint fill on a CTA hover. Winners roll the label, invert to a solid section token, or draw a line.
- A contrasting-accent `border-bottom` under the scrolled nav; frosted-white glass as the default nav surface (two of four verified surfaces use no blur at all).
- One uniform hover repeated across every element class, or a generic `fade-up 20px` on every section.
- A per-letter typewriter headline; a fake timed spinner or fixed-duration progress bar.
- A page-wide cursor blob under `mix-blend-mode:difference`.
- A neon or high-saturation accent — palettes stay two-tone warm-neutral or monochrome — and a fast 1.1×+ image pop.
- A product-card grid above the fold, a "Shop now" hero CTA, urgency, or price-forward copy; stock imagery; a footer that feature-lists.

**Macrostructures it runs.**
- `maison-scroll` — slider hero, lateral swiper procession, emblematic-product beat, tabular footer; commerce hides behind "Discover", zero peak, even restrained intensity. Route here when the brief is an established house with a catalog. (Delvaux, winner-verified)
- `argument-scroll` — 15+ sections arguing to a joining moment, the one late peak fused with the close. Route here when the brief carries an ask — an investment, a venture, a membership. (Son Daven, winner-verified)
- `engine-world` — the engine is the page; never commit without a WebGL path. Pavilion-glide variant: a scored camera procession through self-contained rooms, one hero object per room, one charged gesture each (Cartier, Louis Vuitton Collectibles). Folio variant: a living WebGL ground under a spotlight-dim index (Depo Luxe, winner-verified). Route here when the brief has an object worth walking around.
- Heritage-revival long-scroll — an unbroken vertical spine that never bottoms out plus an infinite horizontal product carousel, the late 3D-object interaction as the only spike. No catalog slug of its own yet: it takes `argument-scroll`'s spine with an infinite vertical track in place of a bottom. Route here when the brief is a revived storied house. (Urban Jürgensen, winner-verified)

**Reflexes — enumerate, then reject by name before committing.**
1. Cream ground, muted gold accent, charcoal ink, a Didot or Bodoni headline: the overexposed premium-consumer cluster, Mocha Mousse included.
2. A centered serif headline over a full-bleed stock still, "Discover" underneath — the luxury costume bolted onto a SaaS template.
3. Whitespace plus one slow fade-up as the whole idea, and nothing moving between hero and footer.
4. `scale(1.05)` with `opacity .7→1` stamped on every card, link and nav item as "the elegant hover".
5. A gold hairline under the scrolled nav, added because a bare bar felt unfinished.
6. "Timeless", "crafted", "heritage", "curated" doing the work a named date, place or count should do.
7. A per-letter typewriter on the house name, offered as ceremony.
8. The bespoke soundscape declared in the plan and never wired to a gesture-unlocked toggle.
