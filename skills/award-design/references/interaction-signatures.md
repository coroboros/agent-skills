# Interaction Signatures — the live substrate

The signature carries the hero; this file keeps the *rest* of the page alive. The dominant build failure it closes is the one a fresh visitor names in one breath — **the page dies after the hero**: a loud hero moment, then static editorial to the footer, every link and image and card inert. A jury reads that as a prototype with a good first slide. Load at Phase 4 alongside `motion-palette.md` and `text-effects.md`; the interaction ingredient-set is committed in the design_plan.

## Two layers — a distributed signature over a live substrate

The winning architecture is **not** one hero climax, and **not** one effect sustained unchanged across the whole scroll. Both were refuted against the award record; the strongest verified quiet/luxury winner (Cartier '365', Awwwards SOTD 7.64 + CSS Design Awards) runs a third model:

- **Layer 1 — a distributed signature.** A small set of bespoke moments, each **tied to the meaning of its own section**, over the scroll — one dominant climax (the hero, `signature-invention.md`) plus two or three quieter section-specific echoes. Cartier ships distinct per-chapter components (jewellery steps assemble on scroll; a chapter mimics a film reel; another splits and reverses its layout) — *not* one reusable effect stamped everywhere. A single generic fade-in on every element is itself an AI-slop tell (`anti-patterns.md`).
- **Layer 2 — a low-amplitude interaction substrate.** Every interactive element responds — link, image, card, control, nav — in **one coherent, quiet vocabulary applied identically everywhere**. This is the connective tissue that keeps the world breathing between the distributed moments.

*Transfer caveat:* Cartier '365' is a chaptered editorial magazine, a different archetype from a single-scroll landing page — the distributed model transfers by inference, not proof. What is proven and binding is Layer 2: the substrate is never optional.

**And a third, quiet channel — the page breathes at rest.** The substrate answers the pointer and the reveals answer the scroll; an award page also carries at least one *ambient* channel that moves with neither — a grain that drifts, a warmth that breathes, a ticker that runs, a signature element idling. A page whose every motion waits for an input reads embalmed between interactions — the fresh visitor names it "not enough micro-animations" even when every element responds on touch. One or two declared idle channels, low amplitude, `prefers-reduced-motion`-guarded; never a zoo. The channel binds at every Motion dial — at Motion ≤3 it is ONE channel at minimum amplitude, not zero. Any idle channel that runs past five seconds carries the WCAG 2.2.2 pause/stop path (motion-palette.md).

**The signature must carry, not merely recur.** The distributed moments and the substrate are not unrelated per-section tricks — they are variations on **one recognizable signature idea**, so a visitor can name a single through-line that runs hero to footer — and at least one signature element *travels* that distance: it persists, accumulates, or progresses section by section (a counter that grows, a bar that loads, a scrubbed transformation). Entrance-only echoes are episodes, not a thread. The hero states it loudest; the section echoes restate it in their own content; the substrate vocabulary carries its DNA (if the signature is *ink developing into permanence*, the hovers ink too; if it is a *lime-then-settle colour pass*, the links settle the same way). "Zero signature carrying through the navigation" — a striking hero over a body that shares none of its language — is a distinct failure from the substrate being absent: the substrate keeps the page *breathing*, the carried signature keeps it *one world*. Terminal Industries is the reference: a single scroll-scrubbed transformation sustained top to bottom (`text-effects.md`, the craft lever). A hero moment with an unrelated, inert body fails this even when every element technically has a hover rule.

**An echo is the mechanic transformed, never an emblem re-placed — binding.** The section echoes are the signature *mechanic* re-expressed in other surfaces' own terms — the nav's hover answers the way the hero moves, a figure responds with the hero's physics, the footer's moment re-racks the climax — in at least **two non-hero sections**. A persistent emblem that follows the scroll (a chip, a rail marker, a badge) is *presence*, and presence alone is not an echo: the campaign's 6.9 build carried its tilt chip and rail ball everywhere while the tilt mechanic itself never left the hero — the user read the thread as missing. The winners' shape (dossier-verified): Aristide runs one face and one slide idiom across loader counter, index titles, explore indicator, and project morphs; Terminal re-fires its loader recolor on mid-page stat counters and grounds the footer in its end-state; Mat Voyce's per-letter roll IS the nav, the section labels, and the footer socials. Each claimed echo is DRIVEN at Phase 5 (§8) and named in the ledger — an echo that cannot be driven as the mechanic transformed is an emblem.

**The spectacle is stateful and replayable.** The committed signature moment names its replay behavior in the design_plan — it differs each run, accumulates, or re-racks (a replay control, a re-deal, a physics field that never plays the same twice); a fire-once spectacle that leaves a static frame after its first play is an entrance, not a signature.

**The middle is layered, never sequential — the dead-middle law.** The 7.2–7.4 build runs its channels one after another — hero climax, a few image reveals, then bare prose to the footer — so the second half of the scroll rides a single spent channel and reads dead on every pass after the first. The alive tier stacks the channels *through* the middle: a scrubbed décor texture runs behind the prose (welded to scroll position, reversible by construction), each content block still enters fire-once and persists, one in-character idle loop keeps breathing, and one mid-scroll moment is operable — something the visitor's own hand drives. Each channel is already law on its own (the `motion-palette.md` split, the idle band above, the substrate palette below); what binds here is the stacking — the channels run together from hero to footer, never hero-only. Score-cited across registers: FlowFest (7.36) beds `scrub:0` rainbow arches and a rotating circle under fire-once loader-timeline entrances, with a marquee and a mouse-follow face mid-page; Son Daven (7.62) runs five scrubbed parallax layers and a scroll-scrubbed reading wash under `once:!0` reveals; Terminal Industries (7.68) drives a scroll-scrubbed canvas frame-sequence behind fire-once char reveals with a radial-glow idle (all winner-verified). A middle whose only motion is entrance reveals has dropped three of the four channels — that is the exact gap between the dead build and the tier.

## Restraint lowers amplitude, never coverage

The register calibrates **how much** each element moves, never **whether** it moves. A quiet build — near-white minimalist, corporate luxury — keeps **full coverage at low amplitude**: everything responds, nothing shouts. Stillness itself is not the craft; a page that strips interaction off its elements to feel "calm" reads inert, not refined. The opposite failure is real too — a busy animation on *every* hover and scroll reads chaotic (`anti-patterns.md` UX). Restraint is the amplitude dial (`atmosphere-calibration.md` Motion), turned low; coverage stays total.

**Low amplitude is not imperceptible — this is the failure that keeps recurring.** "Quiet" means the move is small and unhurried, not that it fails to register. A hover the pointer cannot *feel* — a 1–3% scale, a barely-there tint, a fire-once effect that leaves a static frame — is dead, not restrained, and a fresh visitor names the page as dead. The floor is **perceptibility**: every response must be noticeable to someone actually using the page, even at minimal amplitude, and it is judged by *driving* it (`preflight.md` §8, `audit-rubric.md`) — never by confirming a `:hover` rule exists in the CSS. Calibrate down to quiet; never down to invisible.

- **Minimalist** — coverage total, amplitude minimal but **perceptible**, and **pointer/focus, not scroll-triggered** (Minimalist sits at Motion 3 — `atmosphere-calibration.md` keeps it to `transition: opacity`/`color` on hover/focus, not scroll motion): a hairline underline that clearly draws under a link, a contained figure zoom slow enough to *read* as a move paired with a tint or frame shift, a focus ring at t=0. Quiet is the tempo, not the invisibility — every element responds and the response registers.
- **Corporate Luxury** — the same coverage with a touch more licence (Motion 5): slow tasteful hover reveals, a considered cursor. No parallax pile, no cursor circus.
- **Editorial** — the substrate plus a few reading-tied text-emphasis moments (`text-effects.md`).
- **Immersive / Bold / Experimental** — higher amplitude is licensed, but the *coverage* rule is identical; the loud cinematic tactics of a maximal winner (neon-on-near-black, gesture-3D) never generalize onto a quiet build.

## The interaction ingredient-set — declared, coherent, everywhere

At Phase 4 the design_plan **names the interaction vocabulary** — the same way it names type and color — drawn first from the chosen archetype's *Effect palette* (the evidence-based recipes of that line's winners, at the end of the archetype reference), then this file, `text-effects.md`, and `motion-palette.md`, and derived from the spine. Four rules hold it together:

1. **One vocabulary, applied identically — within a class; distinct across classes.** The same hover language on every image, the same treatment on every link, the same reveal on every secondary — but a button, a link, a figure, and the nav never all answer with one recycled move. Consistency within a class is the luxury; one trick stamped on every class (the fill-sweep on buttons *and* links *and* cards, on this build and the last) is a default in costume, the interaction sibling of the one-fade-for-everything reveal. Compose the set so each element class owns its treatment and the whole still reads as one voice.
2. **Bespoke moments are per-section, not per-element.** The distributed signature moments are content-tied and few; the substrate is uniform and everywhere. Do not stamp one signature effect onto every element, and never ship the same generic fade on all of them.
3. **Subordinate to the signature.** The substrate is quiet by design — it is the tissue, not a second climax. If a substrate move competes with the hero for attention, lower its amplitude.
4. **State colours are committed tokens at full strength.** Every colour a `:hover` / `:focus` / `:active` state paints — a fill, an underline, a text swap — resolves to a DESIGN.md token, and on a control it is the *committed interactive accent*, not its paler sibling. A tint that exists for grounds and washes never becomes the state colour of a button: a CTA sweeping in a washed `oklch(74% …)` fill while every link answers in the committed `oklch(53% …)` accent is **the pale hover nobody chose** — it reads faded, not refined. A colour literal in a state rule that appears in no token sheet is token drift at the exact moment the user is looking.

**The grammar is what makes the variety cohere — the evidence-based law.** Across every archetype's winners the same architecture recurs: each element class carries a *distinct* mechanic — the button floods or presses, the link draws, the figure scales inside its frame, the nav swaps ink — and the page still reads as one voice because a shared grammar sits underneath: one easing family (Cuberto routes fill, roll, underline, and cursor through one expo-out; Delvaux ships a byte-identical `cubic-bezier(0.25,1,0.5,1)` across CSS and GSAP), one scarce accent holding one job, one metaphor (FlowFest: every response is an object on a table — buttons press into their hard shadow, cards tilt, stickers peel). Repeating one mechanic on every class is not consistency, it is the absence of a grammar. The archetype's *Effect palette* lists the verified recipes per class; the design_plan picks one per class and names the grammar that binds them.

**The substrate answers a discrete verb's input — press/strike class only.** Where the signature's verb class is press/strike (`signature-invention.md`, the verb classes), a tap or click on a substrate element is the quiet verb instance — the portrait recoils under the tap, the heading takes the small hit — and inside that scope hover is the approach, the input is the contact. The scope is strict: **drag/steer and scroll/scrub verbs stay on the signature surface** — a drag-verb build never makes its pull-quotes draggable, a scrub-verb build never turns headings into scrubbers; outside the press/strike scope, hover remains a first-class substrate verb, never a lesser channel. **The misfire guard:** the quiet verb response never hijacks an activation — links and buttons keep their action and the verb rides the response's presentation; a scroll-intent tap never triggers. On touch this law IS the invitation's floor: the tap works where a cursor does not. At Phase 5 the input channel is driven on elements the reviewer samples from the detector's substrate census — never the builder's shortlist — with the peak-hold measure so the transient is read at its peak.

**The instrument form and the dual invitation.** Where the pointer performs the verb AND the archetype earns a designed cursor, the affordance takes the **verb's instrument form** — the world's tool (a mallet, a loupe, a chisel), art-directed to the register — because the instrument is the gesture's own affordance; and **the deliberate no-cursor stands where the mechanic IS the pointer** (the canonical experimental winner drives a car — a cursor would fight the physics). The **invitation is dual-path**: fine pointers get the instrument or a motion cue; touch gets a diegetic taught beat (the object's own self-move, a one-time gesture demo) — a cursor-only invitation abandons the touch audience. This is a judgment law held at the assessor boundary: the fresh reviewer, having read no narration, taps and names the gesture within ten seconds.

## Hide-reveal — a secondary is earned, never static

A decorative or atmospheric element sitting beside a primary — a coordinate beside the wordmark, a caption, a meta line — is **never left static**. Its default exit is to cut it; the reveal is the rare exception, not the reflex.

1. **Cut it** (default) — if it adds nothing even when revealed, it was set-dressing. Remove it. Most secondaries end here.
2. **Fold it into an interaction** (rare, justified, subtle) — for the few secondaries that genuinely earn their place: hidden at rest, revealed on hover/press with a *quiet* ingredient from `text-effects.md`. This is an occasional move, never a pattern stamped on every secondary — a page that hides-and-reveals several elements reads as a gimmick. And it is **not itself a marker of award quality**: the wordmark-coordinate reveal is a small, quiet reward, not a signature; justify each use against "would cutting it lose anything?" or it does not ship.

Used sparingly, this converts a genuine secondary — a coordinate that earns a second glance — into a quiet reward. A directional arrow that only appears on a CTA's hover is the same idea: it never sits as resting decoration, and if the CTA reads without it, it is cut, not revealed.

**Beside the nav wordmark: cut — never hide-reveal.** A secondary glued to the logo (an issue line "No. 14 — Summer 2026", a coordinate, a tagline) is *removed*, not tucked into a hover — the wordmark stands alone. The rare justified hide-reveal belongs elsewhere (an in-body caption or coordinate), never on the brand mark. And the wordmark, being a link, carries the same substrate response as every other element: a quiet mark/word treatment on hover and focus. A wordmark that does nothing on hover is a dead element in the spot the eye checks first — a fail, not restraint.

**Touch is mandatory, and content is never trapped.** The hide is a progressive enhancement for fine pointers only — `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` gates the hidden state; a coarse pointer (touch) gets the element in a considered resting state or revealed on a deliberate press. Content is never left permanently unreachable behind a hover that a finger cannot trigger. Verify on a touch emulation, not desktop hover alone.

```css
.coord { opacity: 1; }                              /* touch + no-hover: always reachable */
@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine) {
  .coord { opacity: 0; transform: translateX(-0.4em);
    transition: opacity .3s var(--ease), transform .3s var(--ease); }
  .brand:hover .coord, .brand:focus-visible .coord { opacity: 1; transform: none; }
}
```

## The palette — substrate mechanics

Pick a coherent subset for the ingredient-set; amplitude is set by register. Evidence tags read as in `motion-palette.md` (**winner** / **shipped** / **technique**).

| Mechanic | Amplitude by register | Notes | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Link underline / label draw** (`::after` scaleX or clip, one direction) | all — hairline, quiet | one treatment reused on every link; the `::after` clips to the shape (`anti-patterns.md`) | shipped |
| **Figure hover — perceptible contained zoom + a companion cue** | a *felt* move, not a twitch | the frame stays put; the image scales slowly *inside* its `overflow: hidden`/`clip` bound, smoothly eased on enter **and** leave, paired with a companion cue the pointer reads (a frame tint, a scrim lift, a caption) — the register the user named is "a coloured frame and a zoom-in", a felt response, not a 1–3% scale that reads dead. Never a raw uncontained `:hover{scale}` on a bare `<img>` (the stock tell, `anti-patterns.md`); never a scale that fights a `clip-path` reveal on the same element or ships without a `transition` — that is what makes the image escape its frame with no smooth transition | shipped |
| **Hide-reveal secondary** (coordinate, caption) | all | `@media (hover)` gated, touch-reachable fallback; reveal ingredient from `text-effects.md` | shipped |
| **Directional arrow — appear / translate on hover** | all | never a resting static ornament; the hover echoes the signature gesture, not a stock nudge (`premium-patterns.md`) | technique |
| **Focus ring at t=0** | all | custom `:focus-visible`, never animated in (`ship-ready-floor.md`) | shipped |
| **Custom or considered cursor** | quiet: none/minimal; loud: bespoke | never the native `grab`/`not-allowed` as an affordance (`anti-patterns.md`) | shipped |
| **Magnetic / tilt element** | loud registers only | Framer `useMotionValue` off the render cycle; not on a minimalist build | technique |
| **Section-tied bespoke moment** (a distributed-signature echo) | per archetype | one per relevant section, content-derived, not one effect reused | winner — Cartier '365' (distributed) |

## Reduced motion and the still state

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, the substrate strips transitions and keeps state — a hover still changes color, it just does not ease; a reveal shows its final state. Coverage survives reduced motion; only amplitude goes to zero. Every hover-revealed secondary is reachable without a pointer (the touch fallback above doubles as the reduced-motion and keyboard path).

Richness stays inside the budget by construction, the way winners scope it: cursors and magnetic elements are desktop-gated (killed on small screens), idle channels are IntersectionObserver-gated to in-view, scroll textures are width-gated where they tax paint — density and the INP/fps budget coexist because every channel is scoped, never because channels are cut.
