# Copy Recipes

Winner copy decomposed into committable structure — headline shapes, CTA families, concrete anchors, and voice mechanics read live from 22 award winners (Awwwards SOTD/SOTM/SOTY, FWA, CSSDA) and re-verified against the served pages. Three laws govern this file. The formula law: "A voice formula is structural, never lexical — its slots are filled only from the build's world-lexicon; a formula filled with category words is a template." The calibration law: "Quoted to calibrate, never to ship — imitate the specificity (the named place, the count, the refusal), never the wording." The language law: "Copy ships in English unless the brief's exact ask names another language — one language, chosen once, page-wide; the brief's own language is never the ask." Every quote below is scanner-banned from shipping `(scanner: QUOTED-EXEMPLAR)`.

## The desire arc — the five answers the content owes

Before any shape below is chosen, the page owes five answers in its *content*: why this exists, for whom, who is already there, what makes it exceptional, why come now. Each is a named section with real material behind it, never a tone — the arc is committed in the direction contract's STORY block, which is why it is decided before a headline is written. A landing missing one of the five reads informative and inert (`anti-patterns.md`).

**The hero leads with the promise, never the category description.** A description names what the thing is — the venue, the discipline, the product class; a promise names where the reader is taken. The A2, A3 and A5 forms below are promise shapes and the U forms are description shapes: the hero draws from the first group, the section under it from the second. The make-or-break surface is no place to spend a label.

## Headline structures by section job

Four funnel jobs — attention, understanding, proof, close. Each form is a slot structure; fill the slots from the world-lexicon only.

### Attention
- **A1 — Name-card: NAME → ROLE → PLACE, verb refused.** *"Gabriel Contassot" / "FREELANCE DESIGN DIRECTOR"* · *"Aristide Benoist — Independent developer"* · *"Type designer & animator, working for brands globally." / "UK based"* (Mat Voyce). Role and place do the differentiating; refusing the verb reads as confidence.
- **A2 — Imperative invitation.** *"Réveillez votre croissance"* (13g) · *"Load Norris"* (pun-imperative hero CTA) · *"Please drive around to learn more about me and discover the many secrets of this world."* (Bruno Simon). One verb aimed at the reader turns the hero into an act.
- **A3 — New-era / reinvention declarative.** *"We have reinvented the future of logistics through the yard."* (Terminal) · *"We create 3D visual storytelling and interactive web experiences that help brands stand out"* (Lusion) · *"Global digital design studio partnering with brands and businesses…"* (Exo Ape). A claimed shift, with the domain named.
- **A4 — Aphoristic declarative (luxury / mission).** *"Beauty is the symbol of the morally good"* (Brunello Cucinelli) · *"AI research and products that put safety at the frontier"* (Anthropic). A principle, no product noun — the brand as worldview.
- **A5 — Refusal / negation-of-category.** *"We do not chase trends or produce work that looks like everyone else."* (Lusion) · *"…be kind to skin and the planet – no exceptions!"* (Truekind) · *"And don't break anything!"* (Bruno). Defining by what it refuses is the cheapest anti-template move.
- **A6 — Title-card (narrative).** *"Ponpon Mania" / "By Patrick HENG & Justine Soulié" / "read now"* · *"True to Oneself" / "kind to Nature"* (Truekind wordplay). The hero is a cover, not a pitch: title, authorship, entry verb.

### Understanding
- **U1 — Verb-first does-what.** *"A fast and flexible JavaScript library to animate the web."* (Anime.js) · *"AI-native technology that turns manual tasks into connected missions."* (Terminal). Plain action, zero adjectival fog.
- **U2 — Named-inputs list.** *"Agence de design de marque 100% créative : branding, stratégie, site et web app, identités visuelles."* (13g) · *"TYPE DESIGN & ILLUSTRATION" · "TYPE ANIMATION" · "MOTION DESIGN"* (Mat Voyce). The offer as real nouns, colon- or comma-separated.
- **U3 — Spec-as-headline.** *"Exat consists of a whopping 1715 glyphs per style"* / *"Two scripts, over 430 languages"* (Exat). The number carries the claim.

### Proof
- **P1 — Named person + role + org.** *"…this is a significant milestone in the race to modernize the yard."* — Karen Jones, Head of New Product, Ryder System, Inc. (Terminal). Full attribution or no testimonial.
- **P2 — Textured stat block.** *"+130"* Collaborations menées avec succès · *"+76"* Marques créées · *"+10"* Années d'activité (13g). Round-but-specific, each labeled.
- **P3 — Attributed roster.** *"Charles Gachet-Dieuzeide, Co-fondateur chez Flying Secoya"* and three more names with real titles (13g). A wall of attributed voices.
- **P4 — Attributed critic pull-quotes.** *"Pure cinematic beauty"* — SIFF Jury · *"Brave, poetic, liberated"* — E-Walla (Siena). Short praise, named outlet.
- **P5 — Heritage-as-proof.** *"The Oldest Fine Leather Goods House in the World"* + *"Since 1829"* (Delvaux) · *"Since 2010, we have been helping our clients…"* (Cuberto). A date plus a defensible record.

### Close
- **C1 — Forward declarative + one imperative.** *"The yard of the future starts today."* → *"Take charge of your yard"* (Terminal) · *"Is Your Big Idea Ready to Go Wild?"* → *"Let's talk"* (Lusion). One future claim, one act.
- **C2 — Warm sign-off.** *"See you there!"* (FlowFest) · *"Always open for new projects and collaborations"* → *"CONTACT MAT"* (Mat Voyce). The close as a handshake.
- **C3 — Availability as trust cue.** *"Available October 2026"* (Cyd) · *"Available for freelance projects:"* (Stefan Vitasović). A status line closes harder than a promise.

## CTA labels

1–3 words, a verb plus the site's *own* noun; single words are common; four-plus only for scheduling flows. Families, verbatim:

- **Invite / reveal:** "Explore All Products", "Browse all work", "Step inside the experience".
- **Contact / talk:** "Tell us", "Let's talk", "CONTACT MAT", "Programmer une visio".
- **Enter / start:** "Enter", "Enter without sound", "read now", "Load Norris", "Start animating".
- **Acquire:** "Get Font →", "Buy Tickets", "npm i animejs" — the install command as the primary CTA.
- **Learn / read:** "SEE CASE STUDY", "View Designs", "What we do", "Try Claude".

Never in a winner hero: "Submit", "OK", "Click here", "Get Started Now!", "Learn More »". Generic "Learn more" survives only past the fold; CTAs collapsing to "[noun] details" (≈8 on Terminal's homepage, beside "Benefit 01/02/03") is feature-index decay — discipline is measured at the fold that lands the award.

## The specificity floor

The countable rule: **every headline and subhead carries ≥1 concrete from the build's world; category-words-only fails.** Six anchor classes, quoted:

- **Named place** — "from Amsterdam, the Netherlands" (Cyd), "based in madrid, spain" (Eloy), "at 14islands, Stockholm" (Stefan), "901 N Pitt Street, Alexandria VA, 22314" (Cuberto).
- **Date** — "Since 1829" (Delvaux), "mclaren f1 since 2019" (Lando), "Awwwards Jury member since 2020" (Stefan).
- **Count** — "1715 glyphs per style" (Exat), "+130 / +76 / +10" (13g), "Max 30 characters" and "(max 30)" (Bruno).
- **Material / medium** — "Fine Leather Goods" (Delvaux), "100% Transparent Formulas" / "Only Verified Ingredients" (Truekind), "steel drums, pints" (FlowFest).
- **Gesture** — "Please drive around" (Bruno), "Hold and drag to navigate the content" (Siena).
- **Refusal** — "no exceptions!" / "Hide Nothing." (Truekind), "Enter without sound" (Rogier), "No slur!" (Bruno).

## Voice mechanics and refusals

- **Person.** First-person singular for solo portfolios (*"My name is Bruno Simon, and I'm a creative developer (mostly for the web)."*). Brand-subject or first-plural for studios and institutions. Second-person imperative for CTAs and event copy only. Registers never mix inside a section.
- **Language.** English by default — the copy language is a client decision, never an inheritance: a brief written in French still yields English copy unless it explicitly asks. When another language is the ask it is total — nav, microcopy, form errors, meta — declared in the DESIGN.md and carried page-wide `(scanner: COPY-LANG)`. A non-English word survives only as a named register device (the house's "Maison", a dish, a place name), never a sentence: sentences from the brief's language surfacing mid-page are conversation bleed — the model writing in its working language instead of the deliverable's. The cheapest tell is data chrome: month names, spec strings, or row states in the brief's language beside English UI text ("Janvier … delivered") — dates and labels hold the page language.
- **Length.** Bimodal: one medium mechanism sentence, then a two-to-four-word punch. Heroes are one sentence, often a fragment; refusals and sign-offs are ultra-short.
- **Punctuation signatures.** The em-dash role tag (*"Aristide Benoist — Independent developer"*). The colon-list offer. Ampersand over "and" in role pairs (*"Type designer & animator"*). The exclamation only on playful or warm paths — never in an error or a serious close.
- **Refusals — what winner copy never says.** No feature bullets in the hero — one promise. No self-narration ("welcome to my award-winning site"); award proof lives in chrome, never announced. No category description as the headline ("A website for filmmakers") — the specific verb, place, count, or material instead.

## Microcopy

Voice carried into chrome — quoted:

- **Interaction instructions:** "Scroll to unlock the immersive film experience" (Siena); "I'm stuck!" / "Teleports you to the closest respawn" (Bruno).
- **Consent in-voice:** "Hey you ✨ This site uses cookies to measure the traffic." (Ponpon Mania).
- **Forms + honest errors:** "Stay in the loop" / "Something went wrong. Please try again later or email me directly at julian@animejs.com" (Anime.js) — the error names the failure and gives a fallback address.
- **Clock / locale badges:** "12:23pm (GMT-4)" · "NYc" (Dondre), "[timezone:gmt+2]" · "40.416775 // -3.703790" (Eloy).
- **Footer credits:** "Design by Uncommon" (Mat Voyce), "Built by Lusion with ❤️", "© 2026 Julian Garnier" (Anime.js).
- **Empty / loading:** "Your cart is empty" (Truekind), "Loading..." (Rogier).
- **FAQ in-voice:** "What should I bring?" → "Good vibes and layers." (FlowFest).

## Anti-signals

The copy tells that mark a template draft, each the negative of a quoted winner move `(scanner: CLICHE-COPY)`:

1. **Distribution-default openers.** No winner opens on "Unleash your X", "Supercharge", "Empower your", "Elevate your", "Built for the modern team", "In today's digital landscape", "Reimagine the way", "Next-generation", "Seamlessly", or "Where A meets B". A hero touching none of the six anchor classes above is the strongest tell.
2. **Abstract rule-of-three.** The triplet fails when its three items are abstractions with no noun from the world; winner triplets are concrete: "Be true" · "Be strong" · "Be bold" (13g), "Clean, Conscious, Performance skincare." (Truekind). Test the items, never the pattern.
3. **Self-narration / category description.** "Welcome to my portfolio, an award-winning creative studio that…" — the name-card states role + place and stops.
4. **Generic CTA sprawl.** "[noun] details" repeated and "Benefit 01/02/03" — the feature-index decay the winner hero never shows.
5. **"Oops!" and exclamation in serious paths.** Winners keep "!" playful; errors are honest and name a way forward.
